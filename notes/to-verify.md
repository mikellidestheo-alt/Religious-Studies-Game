# To verify

Things a teacher or subject specialist should check before students rely on them. Add to this list at each stage. Tick items off when checked.

## General note on sources

The Edexcel 4RS1 specification's indicative content describes Christianity in general terms. It does not set out a specifically Catholic position on each topic. The "correct" answers in this game come from the **Catechism of the Catholic Church (CCC)** and standard Catholic teaching, so they draw on general knowledge beyond the specification. Wrong options that are labelled as Protestant, Anglican, Humanist and so on are broad, simplified summaries. Views differ inside every one of those groups.

Scripture references give the book, chapter and verse only. Wording in feedback paraphrases the passage; it does not quote any translation, except for the very short phrases noted below.

## Stage 1 (12 sample dilemmas)

- [ ] **CCC paragraph numbers** in `sources` fields: CCC 80-82, 309-314, 538-540, 1182, 1250-1252, 1261, 1383, 1438, 1577, 1629, 1650, 1776-1785, 2266, 2267, 2276-2279, 2334, 2402-2406, 2415-2418. These are given from memory of the CCC's structure. Each should be checked against the official text (vatican.va).
- [ ] **p1s3-capital-01**: CCC 2267 was revised in 2018 under Pope Francis to call the death penalty "inadmissible". Please confirm the date and wording. The feedback says "many atheists and Humanists also oppose capital punishment". I believe Humanists UK opposes the death penalty, but this needs checking.
- [ ] **p1s2-annulment-01**: the wrong-option note says the Church of England allows remarriage after divorce in some cases, at the priest's discretion (I understand this has been so since 2002). Also: "divorced Catholics who have not remarried can receive Communion". This is standard teaching, but please check the wording suits your school.
- [ ] **p1s2-euthanasia-01**: the teaching that refusing "burdensome" treatment and giving pain relief that may shorten life are permitted (CCC 2278-2279). Check that the simplified wording is acceptable.
- [ ] **p2s2-lent-01**: "the strict fasting rules are for adults". Under general canon law, fasting binds ages 18-59 and abstinence from meat from age 14. Rules differ between bishops' conferences (for example, Friday abstinence in England and Wales). The feedback keeps this deliberately general.
- [ ] **p1s4-wealth-01**: the phrase "the love of money" (1 Timothy 6:10) is a short phrase common to most English translations. The camel saying in the scene is the penitent's own words, not a quotation from a set translation. The label "prosperity gospel" is a simplification.
- [ ] **p1s4-women-01**: "several women saints are honoured as great teachers of the faith" refers to the women Doctors of the Church (for example, St Teresa of Avila and St Catherine of Siena). The wrong-option note says the Church of England and the Methodist Church ordain women.
- [ ] **p1s1-stewardship-01**: Laudato Si' (2015), Pope Francis. Its description of the Earth as "our common home" is paraphrased, not quoted.
- [ ] **p2s1-conscience-01**: "many Baptists" hold sola scriptura. This is a broad generalisation.
- [ ] **p2s3-altar-01**: the claims that "many Protestant churches put the pulpit at the centre" and that Quaker meeting houses are plain are both generalisations.

## Stage 2: Paper 1 Section 1

- [ ] **p1s1-creation-science-01**: Fr Georges Lemaître, a Belgian Catholic priest, first proposed what became the Big Bang theory (1927). I am confident of this, but it should be checked before it is taught. Also check CCC 159 and 282-289.
- [ ] **p1s1-reincarnation-01**: the wrong-option note says 'soul sleep' is held by Seventh-day Adventists. Check CCC 1013 and 1021-1022 for one earthly life and immediate judgment.
- [ ] **p1s1-trinity-01**: the wrong-option note says the Arian view is close to Jehovah's Witnesses' beliefs. This is a simplification, and should be worded sensitively.
- [ ] **p1s1-predestination-01**: the wrong-option note says double predestination is "close to the view of John Calvin, held in some Reformed churches". This is a simplification. 'Open theism' is labelled as a minority Protestant view.
- [ ] **p1s1-ignorance-01**: CCC 1857-1860 (conditions for mortal sin; ignorance can reduce responsibility). Check that the paragraph numbers match.
- [ ] **p1s1-doubt-01**: CCC 2088 (voluntary and involuntary doubt) and CCC 162 (perseverance in faith).
- [ ] **p1s1-unbelief-01**: CCC 847 (those who, through no fault of their own, do not know Christ). The wording is a simplification.
- [ ] **p1s1-freewill-fate-01**: Sirach 15:14-15 (God left humans in the power of their own free choice). This book is in Catholic Bibles but not in most Protestant ones.
- [ ] Other references used: Genesis 1:27; 2:7; Luke 12:15; Luke 13:1-5; Luke 15:11-32; Romans 3:23; 1 Timothy 2:4; Psalm 139:1-4; Matthew 10:29-31; Matthew 28:19; Mark 9:24; Hebrews 9:27; Exodus 20:15; 1 Corinthians 6:19-20. The game gives these references only. The short phrase "temple of the Holy Spirit" (1 Corinthians 6:19) is common to most English translations; nothing else is quoted.

## Stage 2: Paper 1 Section 2

- [ ] **p1s2-purgatory-01**: 2 Maccabees 12:44-46 (praying for the dead) is in Catholic Bibles but not in most Protestant ones. Check CCC 1030-1032.
- [ ] **p1s2-hell-01**: CCC 1033-1037 (hell as self-exclusion; God predestines no one to hell).
- [ ] **p1s2-faith-works-01**: 'sola fide' is a simplified label for the Protestant position. Lutheran-Catholic dialogue (the 1999 Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification) found much common ground. Check CCC 1987 and 2007-2011.
- [ ] **p1s2-abortion-01**: Jeremiah 1:5 is used as support for the sanctity of life before birth. Check CCC 2270-2275. The scene deliberately avoids mentioning any time limits or legal details.
- [ ] **p1s2-same-sex-respect-01**: CCC 2358 says people with same-sex attraction must be accepted with "respect, compassion and sensitivity". The feedback paraphrases this.
- [ ] **p1s2-same-sex-marriage-01**: the wrong-option note says the Methodist Church in Britain conducts same-sex weddings (it voted to allow them in 2021). Please check. The game avoids the 2023 document *Fiducia Supplicans* on blessings, because its interpretation is debated.
- [ ] **p1s2-separation-safety-01**: CCC 2383 (separation can be legitimate; civil divorce may be tolerated). The feedback directs the person to the police or a domestic abuse service. Check that this suits your school's safeguarding guidance.
- [ ] **p1s2-remarriage-01**: CCC 1650-1651 (the divorced and remarried are not separated from the Church). The question deliberately avoids receiving Communion.
- [ ] **p1s2-contraception-01**: Humanae Vitae (1968). The Anglican Lambeth Conference accepted contraception in some circumstances in 1930.
- [ ] **p1s2-ivf-01** and **p1s2-genetic-engineering-01**: CCC 2374-2379 and Dignitas Personae (2008), which allows somatic gene therapy and opposes enhancement and the destruction of embryos. Check that the simplified wording is acceptable.
- [ ] **p1s2-purposes-marriage-01**: at a Catholic wedding the couple are asked whether they will accept children lovingly from God. This is a paraphrase of the Rite of Marriage.
- [ ] Other references used: 1 Corinthians 15:42-44; Ephesians 2:8-10; James 2:14-17; Mark 1:15; Luke 17:20-21; John 18:36; Luke 23:39-43; Mark 10:6-12; Genesis 1:28; Exodus 20:12; Matthew 19:12; 1 Corinthians 7:32-35. The single word 'dead' from James 2:17 is the only quotation.

## Stage 2: Paper 1 Section 3

- [ ] **p1s3-just-war-01**: CCC 2307-2314 (legitimate defence; Just War conditions in CCC 2309; soldiers as servants of peace in CCC 2310; refusing evil orders in CCC 2313). Quakers are named as pacifists. The feedback lists the Just War conditions in simplified form; check them against your textbook's list.
- [ ] **p1s3-holy-war-01**: the feedback says Pope John Paul II asked forgiveness in 2000 for wrongs done by Christians in the past (the Day of Pardon, March 2000). Please check.
- [ ] **p1s3-peace-group-01**: Pax Christi is described as an international Catholic peace movement (founded in France in 1945) that works through prayer, education and action, including campaigning against nuclear weapons. The specification allows any Christian peace group; check that Pax Christi matches the one your course teaches.
- [ ] **p1s3-prison-fellowship-01** and **p1s3-street-pastors-01**: both organisations are named in the specification. The descriptions are deliberately general: Prison Fellowship is a Christian charity supporting prisoners and their families, and Street Pastors is an interdenominational scheme of volunteers caring for people at night. Check that they match your textbook.
- [ ] **p1s3-sin-crime-01**: Acts 5:29 (obeying God rather than human authority); CCC 1902-1903 (an unjust law does not bind conscience). This is simplified.
- [ ] **p1s3-magistrate-01**: CCC 2266 is summarised as: a just penalty that redresses the disorder, protects the public and helps the offender reform. Textbooks use the terms retribution, deterrence, protection and reformation; check that the matching is acceptable.
- [ ] **p1s3-capital-humanist-01**: the claim that many Humanists oppose capital punishment on human rights grounds. Humanists UK opposes the death penalty (please confirm).
- [ ] Other references used: Luke 12:13-15; Matthew 5:9; Matthew 5:23-24; Matthew 5:38-39; Matthew 6:12; Matthew 18:21-22; Colossians 3:13; Luke 10:25-37; Matthew 25:35-40; James 3:5-10; Psalm 34:18; CCC 821, 1459, 2302, 2304-2306, 2447, 2843. Nothing is quoted directly.

## Stage 2: Paper 1 Section 4

- [ ] **p1s4-rights-basis-01**: the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948). Check CCC 1929-1933 (respect for the human person). The term 'legal positivism' is used in a wrong-option note.
- [ ] **p1s4-refugees-01**: CCC 2241 (more prosperous nations should welcome foreigners as far as they are able; authorities may set conditions). Leviticus 19:33-34 and Matthew 25:35.
- [ ] **p1s4-workers-rights-01**: Rerum Novarum (Pope Leo XIII, 1891), and CCC 2428 and 2434 (a just wage). The mention of trade unions is a simplification of the Church's support for workers' associations.
- [ ] **p1s4-women-leadership-01**: the claim that women chair parish councils, lead schools and work in dioceses is general knowledge. Only ordination (deacon, priest, bishop) is reserved to men.
- [ ] **p1s4-racism-family-01** and **p1s4-football-racism-01**: CCC 1934-1935 (equal dignity; discrimination is incompatible with God's design); Acts 10:34-35.
- [ ] **p1s4-interfaith-eid-01**: Nostra Aetate (1965) says the Church rejects nothing that is true and holy in other religions, and speaks with esteem of Muslims. The feedback paraphrases this. Check CCC 841-843.
- [ ] **p1s4-ecumenism-communion-01**: CCC 1400 (Eucharistic intercommunion with Reformation communities is not possible). Sharing communion in rare, exceptional cases (for example, danger of death) is not mentioned. The note that 'many Anglicans welcome all baptised Christians to communion' is a generalisation.
- [ ] **p1s4-mixed-faith-marriage-01**: CCC 1633-1637 (mixed marriages and 'disparity of cult'). Permission is properly called a 'dispensation'. The Catholic partner's promise is to do all they can to have children baptised and raised Catholic (canon 1125). The game words this simply.
- [ ] Other references used: Galatians 3:28; Revelation 7:9; Matthew 25:31-46; James 2:15-16; Amos 5:24; John 17:21; CCC 820-821, 830-831, 2443-2449. Nothing is quoted directly.

## Stage 2: Paper 2 Section 1

- [ ] **Choice of 'one important Christian' (spec 1.4).** I chose **St Teresa of Calcutta** (Mother Teresa) for all three 1.4 dilemmas. The specification lets centres choose; if your course studies someone else (for example St Paul or St Peter), these three dilemmas will need replacing.
- [ ] **p2s1-teresa-saints-01** and **p2s1-teresa-example-01**: facts used: she founded the Missionaries of Charity in 1950 in Kolkata (Calcutta), won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979 and was canonised in 2016. The phrase 'the poorest of the poor' is commonly attributed to her.
- [ ] **p2s1-teresa-darkness-01**: her long experience of spiritual darkness became known through letters published after her death (the 2007 collection *Come Be My Light*). The term 'dark night' (from St John of the Cross) is used loosely.
- [ ] **p2s1-incarnation-01**: the Council of Chalcedon (451) defined Christ's two natures. 'Docetism' and 'adoptionism' are named in the wrong-option notes. Check CCC 464-469.
- [ ] **p2s1-bible-books-01**: Catholic Bible: 73 books (46 Old Testament, 27 New Testament); Protestant Bible: 66 books (39 Old Testament). The seven deuterocanonical books are Tobit, Judith, 1 and 2 Maccabees, Wisdom, Sirach and Baruch, plus additions to Esther and Daniel.
- [ ] **p2s1-bible-interpretation-01**: Dei Verbum (1965), paragraphs 11-12 (inspiration; literary forms). Check CCC 105-119.
- [ ] **p2s1-pope-infallibility-01**: the First Vatican Council (1870) defined papal infallibility. Check CCC 891.
- [ ] **p2s1-bishops-priests-01**: Catholic bishops are appointed by the pope. The wrong-option note about governments choosing bishops does not name any country. In the Church of England, the Crown formally appoints bishops; this could confuse students, so the wording avoids it.
- [ ] Other references used: John 1:1-14; Mark 1:40-42; Mark 2:15-17; Luke 19:1-10; John 18:36; 1 Corinthians 15:3-14; Luke 24:36-43; 2 Timothy 3:16; Matthew 16:18-19; 1 Corinthians 12:4-27; Matthew 25:40; 1 Corinthians 13:1-3; Mark 15:34; CCC 120, 138, 638-647, 861-862, 881-882, 897-900, 956-957, 1555-1568, 2683, 2731.

## Stage 2: Paper 2 Section 2

- [ ] **Choice of 'one other place' of pilgrimage (spec 2.2).** I chose **Lourdes**. If your course uses a different place (for example Walsingham or Rome), *p2s2-lourdes-01* will need replacing.
- [ ] **p2s2-lourdes-01**: St Bernadette's visions at Lourdes in 1858. The Church has officially recognised a small number of cures as miracles (about 70, though the exact number changes). The feedback deliberately gives no number.
- [ ] **p2s2-jerusalem-01**: the claim that the Stations of the Cross developed so that people could follow Jesus' journey without travelling to Jerusalem is general knowledge and simplified.
- [ ] **p2s2-epiphany-01**: chalking the door at Epiphany is a traditional house blessing in many countries. It may be unfamiliar in some parishes.
- [ ] **p2s2-easter-01**: the Ascension is celebrated forty days after Easter (Acts 1:3). Some countries move it to the following Sunday.
- [ ] **p2s2-advent-01**: Advent is the four weeks before Christmas and the start of the liturgical year.
- [ ] Other references used: Isaiah 9:2-7; Luke 2:1-20; Matthew 2:1-12; Mark 11:1-11; Mark 14-16; John 19:16-42; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4; James 5:14-15; CCC 524-528, 1168-1169, 2691.

## Stage 2: Paper 2 Section 3

- [ ] **p2s3-real-presence-01**: the wrong-option notes say most Baptists hold a memorial view of communion, and that Lutherans hold Christ is present together with the bread. The scene paraphrases the Lutheran formula 'in, with and under'. Check CCC 1374-1376 (transubstantiation).
- [ ] **p2s3-funeral-ashes-01**: the Church has allowed cremation since 1963. The 2016 instruction *Ad resurgendum cum Christo* (Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith) asks that ashes be kept in a sacred place, not scattered, divided or kept at home. Check CCC 2300-2301.
- [ ] **p2s3-wedding-rite-01**: in the Latin Church the spouses are the ministers of the sacrament (CCC 1623), and the priest or deacon receives their consent for the Church. Marrying outside a church needs permission ('canonical form'). The wording is simplified.
- [ ] **p2s3-adult-baptism-01**: 'RCIA' (Rite of Christian Initiation of Adults). Some countries now call it 'OCIA' (Order of Christian Initiation of Adults). Use the term your school uses.
- [ ] **p2s3-sunday-mass-01**: a Saturday evening Vigil Mass fulfils the Sunday obligation (canon 1248). Check CCC 2180-2181.
- [ ] **p2s3-lectern-01**: the Gospel at Mass is read by a priest or deacon, and lay readers read the other readings. Check CCC 1088 (Christ present when the Scriptures are read).
- [ ] **p2s3-types-prayer-01**: the forms of prayer listed (adoration or praise, thanksgiving, sorrow, petition and intercession) follow CCC 2626-2643, with sorrow added. Check the list against your textbook's terms.
- [ ] Other references used: 1 Corinthians 1:18-23; John 6:51-58; 1 Corinthians 11:23-26; Matthew 6:9-13; Matthew 7:7; Exodus 20:8; Acts 8:14-17; Acts 2:38-41; CCC 1159-1162, 1184-1185, 1213, 1247-1249, 1272, 1285, 1302-1305, 1324, 1349, 1630-1631, 2177-2181, 2700-2704.

## Stage 2: general checks

- [ ] **Penitent names** were chosen for variety and do not refer to real people. 'Mr Haddad senior' (p2s2-jerusalem-01) is deliberately linked to the bakery owner Mr Haddad (p1s4-wealth-01), as a family in the parish.
